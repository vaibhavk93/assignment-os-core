# Tradeoffs

Written at decision time, before the argument for the decision exists. Every row names something that got worse.

| # | What got worse | Who feels it | The alternative that would have avoided it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Discovery breadth collapses to what the user already knows.** The surface will never be the reason someone finds a company they'd never heard of. That is a real loss for a product called *discovery*. | The user, and the product's positioning — "discovery" that only returns known names is a weaker claim. | **A (thematic collections as default)** — editorial groupings introduce unfamiliar names immediately, at the cost of StockFox doing the selecting. |
| 2 | **Familiarity bias gets reinforced by design.** Anchoring the first research on a company the user is already a customer of leans on a documented investing bias (`research_q1.md` gaps names familiarity/home bias explicitly; no India-specific study found, so the cost is real but unsized). | The user, months later, in a portfolio concentrated in consumer names they recognise. | **B (disclosed-criteria sort)** — an objective sort is bias-neutral by construction, at the cost of demanding a criterion the beginner does not have. |
| 3 | **Meter exhaustion slows down.** Optimising for depth on one company instead of breadth across several delays the paywall hit, which is the conversion event (Q3-F2). NYT's own metered research says the looser-engagement / lower-conversion trade is real and directional (Q3-F4). | The business — near-term conversion, and any growth target built on session breadth. | A breadth-first browse surface (**A** or **E**) — more Scorecard opens per session, faster to the meter, and directly into the attention-driven pattern Q4-F5 associates with retail losses. |
| 4 | **A measured double-digit engagement lift is deliberately forgone.** Push notifications and points/prize draws raised trading activity 11% and 12% in the regulator's own study (Q4-F1). Discovery gets none of it. | The product and growth — and the design team, whose shipped gamified pattern language from the learning layer cannot be reused here. | **F (learning-layer-as-discovery)** or **E (social)** — both carry the engagement mechanics natively, and both carry the harm and regulatory exposure the mechanics come with. |
| 5 | **The build now depends on a hand-curated content asset with a permanent maintenance tail.** The brand→listed-entity map is judgement work per row, and it decays — delistings, rebrands, new listings, corporate restructures. | The team, indefinitely. A dataset nobody owns after launch becomes a slow-rotting first impression. | **B** — a pure data sort over exchange data has no editorial surface and no maintenance tail. |
| 6 | **Users whose recognised brands are unlisted hit a dead end at the very first interaction.** Amul is a cooperative, Flipkart is private, Jio sits inside Reliance, Lay's has no Indian listing. This is the single worst-placed failure in the flow. | The subset of first-time users whose consumer world is mostly private companies — plausibly a large subset, and unmeasured until the kill test runs. | **A** — a collection grid cannot dead-end, because every tile is guaranteed to resolve. |
| 7 | **A page of the ≤10 goes to regulatory reasoning.** `intent.md` (#6) rates this the cheapest available differentiator, and that is a bet: an evaluator who reads it as over-lawyering a design brief will see a page of caution where they wanted a page of product. | The candidate, live, in the Friday/Saturday defence. | Leading with the surface design and confining the constraint to a single line — which would forfeit the one signal `intent.md` argues no other candidate will produce. |
| 8 | **The deliverable must state that a discovery surface may already exist in the beta.** Carried per `intent.md` decision 2. It is honest and it visibly weakens the framing — the deck proposes something that might already be shipped. | The candidate. An assumption that reads as diligence to one evaluator reads as "didn't watch the video" to another. | Watching the Loom. Ruled out at `/intent-confirm`; the cost is binding and there is no design that avoids it. |
| 9 | **The strongest challenger was killed on a rule reading we could not verify at primary source.** F died on the ≥3-month-stale-price expectation for educational content, at 0.65 confidence from convergent secondary reporting. If that reading is too broad, the option best matched to this team's EdTech background was cut in error. | The decision itself. This is the most likely place for the recommendation to be wrong. | Reading the primary SEBI circular, or asking StockFox directly — which doubles as one of the ≥2 questions the deliverable owes them. |
