# Hiring Signal Patterns

What companies are actually evaluating in interview assignments, beyond the stated question.

## How to Read a Hiring Signal
The assignment text reveals the surface task. The hiring signal is what the evaluator scores you on — often different. Process: read the JD (what the role needs day-to-day) → read the assignment (what thinking it requires) → read the interviewer profile (what they value) → find the gap between surface task and evaluation criteria.

## Common Evaluation Areas by Assignment Type

**Product/Growth Strategy** — Signals: structured thinking, market understanding, prioritization rigor, tradeoff clarity. Want: a clear "so what," not a list. Failure: 15 unprioritized generic ideas.
**Product Teardown** — Signals: user empathy, analytical depth, business model understanding, POV. Want: a specific, defensible recommendation. Failure: describing features instead of evaluating them; praising everything.
**PRD** — Signals: requirements thinking, edge cases, cross-functional awareness. Want: clear success metrics, rollout, engineering tradeoffs. Failure: a wishlist with no metrics/risks/rollout.
**Metrics/Diagnostics** — Signals: hypothesis-driven thinking, data literacy. Want: a cause tree, explicit hypotheses, what data and why. Failure: listing metrics with no framing.
**Market Research** — Signals: research depth, synthesis quality, strategic implication. Want: insights (pattern + implication), not facts. Failure: listing competitor features with no "so what."
**Experiment Design** — Signals: statistical rigor, guardrail awareness. Want: correct sample size logic, guardrails, staged rollout. Failure: missing guardrails or success threshold.
**Executive Memo** — Signals: executive communication, decisiveness, conciseness. Want: recommendation up front. Failure: too long, too hedged, no clear ask.

## Seniority Signal Calibration
- **APM/Junior:** clear thinking, structure, user empathy — evaluated on logic, not business depth.
- **PM (3–5y):** prioritization rigor, metrics fluency, independent POV — can they handle ambiguity?
- **Senior PM (5–8y):** strategic thinking, business impact framing, tradeoff navigation — do they connect product to revenue?
- **Principal/Director:** portfolio thinking, org-level influence — are they solving the right problem? Can they say no?

## Company Type Signals
Consumer/B2C: user psychology, growth levers, retention — data-informed, move fast. Fintech: trust, compliance, activation friction — rigorous, risk-aware. B2B SaaS: customer success, enterprise buying, NRR — structured, implementation-aware. Marketplace: supply/demand balance, liquidity, network effects — systems thinking. E-commerce/Travel/OTA: conversion, funnel, loyalty, seasonality — A/B culture, commercial acumen. Startup (<100): resourcefulness, speed — opinionated, no fluff.

## What NOT to Optimize For
Pretty output over content. Covering everything over depth on the right things. Sounding impressive over clarity. Agreeing with a wrong premise. Generic frameworks over specific evidence-backed analysis.

## Red Flags (gets a "No")
No clear recommendation (excessive hedging). No numbers/estimates. Unstated assumptions. Ignoring feasibility tradeoffs. No prioritization. Data with no implication. Polish over substance.
