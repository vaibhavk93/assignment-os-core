# Trade-offs

Four design decisions, each with a stated cost or a rejected alternative. None benefit-only.

1. **No composite score or ranking anywhere pre-click.**
   Cost: no one-glance "is this good" shortcut; some users bounce faster without it.
   Rejected alternative: show the score with a disclaimer. Rejected because a number outlives its disclaimer in a user's head. Same logic behind the finfluencer rule that educational content must reference price data at least 3 months old: people anchor on the number, not the fine print.

2. **Browsing is unmetered; only opening a Scorecard spends one of the 3 free analyses.**
   Cost: a determined browser can window-shop indefinitely with zero revenue signal and real serving cost.
   Rejected alternative: meter card views too. Rejected because it recreates the "too many gates before I understand anything" complaint already documented in beginner overwhelm patterns, and because the comparable-product evidence rewards exploration breadth, not gating it.

3. **No engagement gamification (points, streaks, leaderboards) tied to trading-adjacent actions.**
   Cost: a measurably lower short-term engagement lift than competitors get from exactly these mechanics. FCA found ~11–12% higher trade frequency from them.
   Rejected alternative: scope streaks or badges to watchlist or Scorecard actions. Rejected because FCA ties the identical mechanic to a 6–8 percentage-point higher share of trades in riskier products, and the SEC has separately named it a formal scrutiny category.

4. **Peer comparison hidden pre-click, shown only inside the Scorecard and Competitive Lens.**
   Cost: removes a genuinely useful "cheaper than its closest peer" signal that could speed up conviction earlier.
   Rejected alternative: show a one-line peer delta on the card. Rejected because a comparative claim about an unopened card reads closer to "prefer this one" than a standalone fact does. That's precisely the line SEBI's broadened "opinion on securities" definition is drawn around.
