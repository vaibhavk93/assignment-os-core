# Research: Q4 — Gamification/engagement mechanics in trading and investing apps: engagement benefit and documented risk

## Findings

### Finding 1 — Same mechanics that drive engagement measurably drive riskier trading (paired benefit/risk, quantified)
- Claim: UK FCA's own study of trading-app "digital engagement practices" (DEPs) found push notifications raised trade count 11%, points/prize draws raised it 12% — proof the mechanics work as engagement tools. The *same two mechanics* raised the share of trades in risky products (crypto/CFDs) by 8% and 6%; effect was largest for low-literacy, female, and younger users; heavier-DEP-usage accounts earned lower net returns.
- Source: FCA research, reported by Finextra, MoneyWeek, Money Marketing, Which? (2023).
- Type: Regulator study via reputable press (secondary reporting). Confidence: 0.7. Date: 2023.

### Finding 2 — SEC formally named leaderboards/badges/points/streaks as a regulatory concern category
- Claim: SEC's Request for Information on broker-dealer DEPs explicitly lists "games, streaks and contests with prizes; points, badges, leaderboards, visual cues and celebrations for trading" as features under scrutiny for encouraging trading "for platform-revenue reasons, not user benefit."
- Quote: "these features may encourage investors to trade more often, invest in different products, or change their investment strategy" — Gensler.
- Source: SEC.gov press release 2021-167. Type: Primary regulatory document. Confidence: 0.85. Date: Aug 2021.

### Finding 3 — A state regulator brought and settled a formal enforcement action naming "gamification" as the harm
- Claim: Massachusetts Securities Division complaint alleged Robinhood's gamification (confetti, digital scratch tickets, free-stock rewards) caused "unnecessary trading risks," citing an inexperienced customer who made 12,700+ trades in six months. Robinhood settled for $7.5M, agreeing to overhaul its digital engagement practices.
- Source: Cointelegraph, InvestmentNews, WealthManagement.com, Velaw, Axios (converging coverage). Type: Press reporting a regulatory filing/settlement. Confidence: 0.85. Date: complaint Dec 2020, settled Jan 2024.

### Finding 4 — Controlled experiment: gamified UI causally increases risk-taking on volatile assets
- Claim: Chapkovski, Khapko & Zoican (University of Toronto) found users on a gamified interface (post-trade confetti/animation) took significantly more risk than a plain-interface control group, with the effect amplified on higher-volatility securities.
- Source: Investment Executive coverage of the study. Type: Press reporting an academic study (paper not read directly). Confidence: 0.6. Date: reported 2023/2024.

### Finding 5 — Peer-reviewed evidence: gamified/attention-driven retail trading predicts losses
- Claim: Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, "Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users," *Journal of Finance* (2022) — Robinhood's design drives herding into attention-grabbing stocks; heavy Robinhood-user buying in a stock predicts −4.7% average 20-day abnormal returns.
- Source: *Journal of Finance* 77, 3141–3190. Type: Peer-reviewed journal. Confidence: 0.85. Date: 2022.

## Gaps
- FCA and Chapkovski et al. figures sourced via press, not primary report/working paper — multi-outlet convergent but unverified at source.
- No primary SEBI statement found on gamification in *registered/informational* apps specifically — SEBI's documented action targets unauthorized virtual/fantasy trading apps, a related but distinct phenomenon (out of scope per Q4: "does not need to be India-specific").

## Overall Confidence
0.75 — risk side rests on one primary regulator document, one settled enforcement action, and one peer-reviewed journal study, all converging on the same mechanism (engagement feature → more/riskier trading). Benefit side is thinner but Finding 1's own data proves the mechanic works as engagement tool before it causes harm — that pairing is the usable tradeoff.

## Recommendation
sufficient
