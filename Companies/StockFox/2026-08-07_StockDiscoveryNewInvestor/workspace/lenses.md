# Three Lenses

## Product
Explore is a new home surface: a brand-recognition strip, theme rails with a disclosed objective sort, and an unmetered compare tray, replacing what would otherwise default to a search bar. Additive, not a rebuild. The Scorecard, Confidence Journal, Competitive Lens, and learning layer stay exactly as they are; Explore is the on-ramp into them. Buildable as low-fidelity CSS boxes. The real build risk is content operations: someone has to define and keep 4–5 theme rails current with defensible sort criteria. Not engineering.

## Business
Discovery sits at the top of a metered funnel: the first 3 Scorecard opens are free. Every card tap that doesn't convert is serving cost with no revenue signal. StockGro's Stoxo, SEBI-registered-analyst-backed and built on 35M+ users' behavioral data (StockGro's own scale, not StockFox's), is the real competitive threat, chasing the same "confusion into conviction" positioning. The one asset it can't copy quickly is trust: staying rigorously on the right side of the SEBI line while a better-funded competitor doesn't have to.

## User
A first-time investor with zero candidate in mind, likely already burned by a Telegram tip or bounced off a screener. What changes: a starting point that isn't a search box. What they give up: a one-glance verdict. No score, no ranking, no price target before they click. A real loss for someone who wants a fast answer, not a research project.

## Tensions, resolved

**Excitement vs. gamification harm.** The brief wants discovery to feel exciting. FCA, SEC, and Robinhood evidence all tie the mechanics that create that feeling (streaks, points, leaderboards) to measurably riskier trading in less-experienced users. Resolved by gamifying exploration (sectors browsed, cards compared) and never commitment (no streak or points tied to a Scorecard open or adding a stock). Traded away: engagement lift. FCA's own numbers put it at ~11–12% trade frequency. Explore will look less sticky week over week than a leaderboard-driven feed, on purpose.

**Breadth vs. the 3-free-analysis meter.** Encouraging exploration argues for unlimited browsing; the business model argues for gating. Resolved by metering only the Scorecard open. Traded away: a revenue signal on browsing itself. A determined window-shopper can view dozens of cards for free, all cost, zero funnel progress.

**Depth promise vs. the novice ceiling.** Resolved by moving depth, not deleting it: the full 200+ metrics stay inside the Scorecard; the card only earns curiosity. Traded away: a fast "is this good" answer, deliberately absent pre-click, so some users bounce before reaching the depth that was supposedly preserved.
