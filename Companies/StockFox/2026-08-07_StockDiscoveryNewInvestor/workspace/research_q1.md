# Research: Q1 — What behavioral-finance evidence describes how novice retail investors go wrong with stock decisions specifically?

## Findings

### Finding 1 — Overconfidence bias drives excessive trading
- Claim: Investors overestimate own skill (credit skill for wins, luck for losses) and trade excessively as a result; most-active traders earn the lowest net returns after costs. India: an ISB study of 2.5M NSE retail investors, 1.4B trades (Jan 2005–Jun 2006), found overconfidence plus disposition effect jointly explain recurring retail losses.
- Source: Barber & Odean, "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth," J. of Finance 2000 [global]; De/Gondhi/Sarkar, ISB Centre for Analytical Finance, via Business Standard [India].
- Source type: peer-reviewed academic (global); academic study reported by press, not read primary (India).
- Confidence: 0.8 global / 0.6 India.
- Date: 2000 (global, foundational); India data 2005–06, reported 2012 — dated, no newer India large-N replacement found.
- Mechanism: mistaking luck for skill inflates confidence → overtrading → returns erode via costs.

### Finding 2 — Disposition effect: sell winners early, hold losers
- Claim: Investors realize gains readily but avoid realizing losses — cross-country documented; ISB study above names it a dominant driver of Indian retail losses.
- Source: Shefrin & Statman 1985 (coined term); Odean 1998, "Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses?" [global]; ISB study above [India].
- Source type: peer-reviewed academic.
- Confidence: 0.8.
- Date: 1985/1998 global; India data as above.
- Mechanism: loss aversion plus hope of reversal delays exiting losers; gains get locked in early from fear of reversal.

### Finding 3 — Herding/FOMO, and SEBI evidence that novices lose most
- Claim: A 323-respondent Indian retail-investor survey found herding and loss aversion significantly shape investment decisions, FOMO amplifies both. Separately, SEBI's own F&O study found 93% of individual F&O traders lost money FY22–24 (₹1.8 lakh crore aggregate); loss rate was highest for the youngest traders — 93% under-30 vs. 79% over-60 — evidence inexperience correlates with worse, not just different, outcomes.
- Source: "Herding and loss aversion in stock markets... FOMO," Int'l Journal of Emerging Markets (Emerald, India sample); SEBI press release, Sep 2024.
- Source type: peer-reviewed survey (India); primary regulatory source (SEBI).
- Confidence: 0.55 (self-report survey, n=323); 0.85 (SEBI — primary, official, but F&O/derivatives, not cash-equity stock-picking — scope caveat, not disqualifying).
- Date: study ~2022; SEBI data Sep 2024 (fresh).

## Gaps
Familiarity/home bias: only generic global sources found, no India-specific study this pass — omitted as a named finding, available if a 4th pattern is needed. No India cash-equity (non-F&O) large-N study newer than the 2012-reported ISB data was found this session.

## Overall Confidence
0.7 — three named, mechanism-described bias patterns, each grounded in at least one India-specific source, cross-confirmed against foundational global literature.
