# Recommendations Summary
_Written by: Case Builder_

## Central Thesis
Ixigo's international travel problem is an algorithm gap, not a feature gap — its ranking engine is trained on domestic price sensitivity and structurally can't serve international discovery, so the fix is an AI-native personalization/ranking layer (intent classifier + re-ranker + proactive trigger system) that treats visa, forex, and trust signals as ranking inputs, giving concrete architectural form to the "hyper-personalization" pillar Ixigo's own management already promised on its Q2 FY26 earnings call.

## Key Recommendations
1. **Build a three-component AI-native architecture** — (a) an international-intent classifier that scores users as "international-curious" and first-time vs. experienced before they search, (b) a destination/content re-ranker that adds visa-friction-adjusted feasibility and forex-decline risk as ranking features alongside price, (c) a proactive trigger system that pushes ranked, timed notifications on state changes (visa policy shift, price drop, visa status update) — none of which requires a chat interface to deliver value.
2. **Ship two tracks in parallel, not two sequential phases** — Track 1: a narrow, 4-week Bangkok visa-alert feature (notification/ranking-layer only) to capture the live, dated Thailand visa-free removal (May 2026) affecting the highest-volume corridor (2.13-2.5M annual travelers). Track 2: the full architecture, sequenced Dubai (4-6 weeks, easiest regulatory path) → Bangkok (8-12 weeks) → Singapore (12-16 weeks), by increasing partnership/regulatory complexity.
3. **Frame the business case as GMV mix-shift, not cost-parity** — Ixigo's low-CAC domestic moat is irrelevant to this decision; the real argument is converting an acquisition advantage (480M users) into a margin advantage (higher international GMV per booking) in a market growing at 11.4% CAGR, faster than domestic.
4. **Resolve visa/forex data sourcing via partner-for-data, build-the-ranking-layer** — negotiate a visa-data partnership (closing MMT's 2-3 month Atlys-driven head start) while keeping the ranking/personalization layer proprietary, rather than copying MMT's bolt-on feature model.
5. **Import a metrics and monitoring discipline wholesale from global practice** — AUC/NDCG offline pre-launch, staged pCTR/pCVR A/B tests per architecture layer, booking-conversion-lift and GMV-mix business metrics, task completion rate (not NPS) for user success, and standing drift monitoring post-launch — because Ixigo has no existing internal practice to extend.

## Priority Order + Rationale
Track 1 (Bangkok visa-alert) ships first and fastest because it is low-effort (a ranking/notification-layer change, not a booking-flow rebuild), high-relevance (a live, dated, news-worthy pain point), and self-contained (does not require the full architecture or a new partnership to work). Track 2 starts immediately in parallel, sequenced by regulatory/partnership complexity (Dubai → Bangkok → Singapore) rather than by pain-point urgency, because the full architecture's durability depends on validating the hardest components (intent classifier, re-ranker) in the simplest regulatory environment first. By the time Track 2 reaches Bangkok, Track 1's live feature there has already generated real conversion data that de-risks the full rollout.

## Business Case Summary
Fund this now because three forcing functions converge in this quarter: (1) Ixigo's own investor guidance already shows international bookings outgrowing domestic, (2) Thailand's visa-free removal for Indians (May 2026) creates a dated, high-volume, high-visibility forcing function in the single largest target corridor, and (3) MakeMyTrip's Atlys-powered visa advantage is a 2-3 month structural head start that widens every quarter Ixigo waits. The $36.5M already earmarked from the Prosus round for AI-platform development is the funding vehicle already in place — what's missing is the architecture and sequencing decision, which this case provides.
