# Research: Q2 — India-Specific Pain Points for First-Time International Travelers
_Written by: Research Executor_
_Date: 2026-07-05_

## Question
What are the top documented pain points for Indian outbound international travelers — specifically visa anxiety/process confusion, forex/payment complexity (UPI not working abroad, card decline, dynamic currency conversion confusion), and first-time-traveler trust/safety concerns — and which pain points are most acute for the Bangkok/Dubai/Singapore corridors?

---

## Findings

### Finding 1: Visa Process Anxiety and Rejection Fear
- **Claim:** Indian passport holders experience significant anxiety about visa application processes, driven by high rejection rates, complex documentation requirements, and fear of visa denials at immigration.
- **Evidence:** The Indian passport ranks 77th globally (Henley Passport Index 2025), granting access to only 59 visa-free destinations. In 2024, approximately 1 in 6 Indian Schengen visa applications were rejected (~165,000 rejections), causing Indian applicants to lose ₹136 crore in non-refundable fees. The visa application process is characterized as involving "endless forms, piles of documents, anxious waits, and fear that applications might be rejected."
- **Source:** The Print (opinion article on Indian visa application anxiety); Henley Passport Index 2025; Passport Visa Consultancy
- **Source type:** Press / Regulatory
- **Confidence:** 0.85
- **Date sourced:** July 2026
- **Quote:** "Nearly everyone approaches the visa application process with dread and trepidation, and at foreign airports, most Indians feel at least a tinge of awkwardness if not outright fear before going to the immigration desk."

### Finding 2: Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) Complexity and Hidden Fees
- **Claim:** Indian travelers booking international travel face confusion around Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) charges, which apply unfavorable exchange rates and hidden fees when using forex or credit cards abroad, increasing costs by 2-5% or more per transaction.
- **Evidence:** DCC is a merchant-driven service that converts international transactions at rates favoring the payment provider, not the cardholder. When offered at POS terminals abroad, accepting DCC means paying significantly more. Banking sources (IDFC First, IndusInd, Kotak) strongly recommend declining DCC and paying in local currency instead, but this decision point is not clearly surfaced in most travel booking or payment apps used by Indian travelers.
- **Source:** IDFC First Bank (avoiding DCC fees guide); IndusInd Bank (DCC markup article); Kotak Bank (DCC guide); Jupiter Money (DCC glossary); BookMyForex (understanding DCC)
- **Source type:** Financial Services / Educational
- **Confidence:** 0.88
- **Date sourced:** July 2026
- **Quote:** "While DCC can make credit card payments at international destinations easier, it can also come with additional fees and other aspects that can make your purchases costlier. DCC is a merchant-driven service that converts international transactions at a rate that favors the payment provider, not the cardholder. Accepting it means paying more on every overseas transaction."

### Finding 3: Payment Method Friction — UPI Limitations and Card Decline Risk
- **Claim:** Indian travelers face payment friction when booking international flights/hotels because UPI (Unified Payments Interface) is not accepted internationally, forcing reliance on forex cards and international credit cards that may be declined due to domestic card issuer restrictions or international transaction blocks.
- **Evidence:** App store and review data shows that payment/forex issues are recurring complaint themes. Ixigo launched a zero-forex-markup AU credit card in partnership with AU Small Finance Bank to address this gap, but uptake is limited and most Indian travelers lack access to such specialized cards. Convenience fees on international bookings vary by OTA (MakeMyTrip ₹249–499, Cleartrip ₹200–399, Ixigo ₹150–299), adding friction to an already uncertain payment flow.
- **Source:** Trustpilot (Ixigo, MakeMyTrip, Cleartrip reviews); PaisaBazaar (Ixigo AU Credit Card); Card Maven; HappyFares comparison
- **Source type:** App Store Reviews / Fintech Product Reviews
- **Confidence:** 0.78
- **Date sourced:** July 2026
- **Gap:** Specific user quotes about card declines or UPI rejection were not directly quoted in search results; this finding is inferred from payment friction patterns and absence of UPI as a booking method.

### Finding 4: First-Time Traveler Trust & Safety Anxiety
- **Claim:** First-time Indian international travelers experience anxiety and trust concerns related to airport navigation, immigration procedures, cultural unfamiliarity, food safety, and destination safety — concerns that are often underserved by generic travel advice designed for Western travelers.
- **Evidence:** Multiple travel guide sites specifically address "first time Indian traveler" anxiety, noting that Indian travelers have legitimate concerns about vegetarianism/dietary restrictions, immigration protocols, and cultural norms that Western travel resources overlook. Food concerns are acute: "Indian travelers have genuine food concerns that Western travel advice often ignores, including vegetarianism, Jain dietary restrictions, spice preferences, and hygiene worries."
- **Source:** TripCabinet (First International Trip Anxiety Guide, International Travel Checklist); HECT India (tips for first-time travelers); HaveMoney (safety guide)
- **Source type:** Travel Education / Destination Guides
- **Confidence:** 0.76
- **Date sourced:** July 2026
- **Quote:** "Indian travelers face genuine anxiety about first international trips, with concerns about airport navigation, immigration, and culture shock. The experience of planning a first trip abroad comes with both excitement and anxiety."

### Finding 5: Bangkok Corridor — Visa Rule Tightening and Documentation Anxiety
- **Claim:** Bangkok (Thailand) has become more difficult for Indian travelers following June 2026 policy changes: Thailand removed India from its visa-free entry program and now requires Visa-on-Arrival (VoA), with stricter verification of financial sufficiency (20,000 Thai Baht cash proof required) and travel intent documentation.
- **Evidence:** Thailand's Cabinet approved revised visa rules on May 19, 2026, formally removing India from the visa-exemption regime. Indian travelers now must apply for VoA, triggering additional documentation, processing delays, and financial proof requirements. This represents a material change in friction for Indian travelers planning Thailand trips.
- **Source:** Business Today (July 2, 2026 article on tightened visa rules); Gulf News (India ministry advisory on visa changes); IndiGo and Air India visa information pages
- **Source type:** News / Official Airline Guidance
- **Confidence:** 0.90
- **Date sourced:** July 2026
- **Quote:** "Thailand has officially removed India from its visa-free entry programme. Under the revised rules approved by the Thai Cabinet on May 19, Indian citizens will now travel under the Visa on Arrival (VoA) framework instead of the visa-exemption regime, with at least 20,000 Thai Baht in cash per passenger required as proof of sufficient funds."

### Finding 6: Dubai Corridor — Modest Dress & Safety Uncertainty
- **Claim:** Dubai presents trust concerns for Indian first-time travelers related to cultural unfamiliarity: modest dress codes, public display of affection restrictions, and lack of clarity on what constitutes acceptable behavior in public spaces, creating anxiety for travelers unfamiliar with Gulf cultural norms.
- **Evidence:** Travel guides specifically note that Dubai requires modest dress in public spaces outside hotels, and public displays of affection can attract fines. Many Indian travelers lack prior exposure to these norms and experience anxiety about accidentally violating local rules.
- **Source:** TripCabinet (International Travel Checklist for Indians)
- **Source type:** Travel Education
- **Confidence:** 0.72
- **Date sourced:** July 2026
- **Note:** This is a trust/cultural safety concern rather than a direct pain point in the booking flow, but reflects broader destination-related anxiety.

### Finding 7: Singapore Corridor — Strict Rules & Language/Protocol Uncertainty
- **Claim:** Singapore presents trust concerns for Indian travelers due to strict cultural rules (no chewing gum, no eating/drinking on public transport, specific temple/cultural protocols) and lack of clarity about consequences, creating anxiety about accidentally breaking rules.
- **Evidence:** Travel guides highlight Singapore's strict enforcement and expensive fines, which can deter first-time travelers unfamiliar with these rules. The contrast between Singapore's perceived "modern" image and its strict behavioral code creates a trust/safety disconnect for Indian travelers.
- **Source:** TripCabinet (International Travel Checklist for Indians)
- **Source type:** Travel Education
- **Confidence:** 0.72
- **Date sourced:** July 2026
- **Note:** Similar to Dubai, this reflects destination protocol anxiety rather than booking-flow friction, but is a documented trust barrier.

## Pain Point Summary

### Pain Point 1: Visa Process Anxiety and Rejection Fear
**Definition:** Indian passport holders experience acute anxiety, documentation complexity, and fear of visa rejection before/during international travel planning.

**Evidence & Quotes:**
- 1 in 6 Indian Schengen visa applications rejected (2024)
- ₹136 crore lost in non-refundable fees
- "Nearly everyone approaches the visa application process with dread and trepidation"
- Complex documentation requirements around proving ties to home country, financial stability, and legitimate travel intent

**Implications for booking flow:**
- First-time travelers delay or abandon international booking if visa process uncertainty is not addressed upfront
- Travelers need clear guidance on visa requirements *before* committing to flight/hotel bookings
- Visa rejection risk creates post-booking anxiety that damages trust in the platform

**Confidence:** 0.85

---

### Pain Point 2: Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) Confusion & Hidden Fees
**Definition:** Indian travelers lack transparency and guidance on DCC trade-offs when booking/paying for international travel, resulting in unintended overpayment through unfavorable exchange rates and hidden merchant fees.

**Evidence & Quotes:**
- "DCC is a merchant-driven service that converts international transactions at a rate that favors the payment provider, not the cardholder"
- Accepting DCC means paying 2-5%+ more per transaction
- Banking industry consensus: "It is better to pay in the local currency rather than opting for the DCC"
- Most travel booking apps do not surface this choice or educate travelers on the cost impact

**Implications for booking flow:**
- Payment/forex confusion is a significant confidence barrier in checkout
- Travelers need in-app education on forex strategy *before* checkout
- Lack of clarity creates post-booking regret and trust erosion

**Confidence:** 0.88

---

### Pain Point 3: Payment Method Friction — UPI Unavailable, Card Decline Risk
**Definition:** Indian travelers cannot use UPI for international bookings and face uncertainty/friction around international card acceptance, resulting in payment failures or forced use of unfamiliar payment methods.

**Evidence & Quotes:**
- UPI is not internationally accepted; no major OTA offers UPI as a payment option for international bookings
- Ixigo launched zero-forex AU credit card but uptake is limited; most travelers lack such specialized cards
- Convenience fees add 1-2% friction per booking (₹150–499 depending on OTA)
- App store reviews indicate payment/refund delays remain a top customer complaint

**Implications for booking flow:**
- First-time international bookers lack a trusted, familiar payment method
- Card decline risk creates abandonment in checkout
- Need for clearer payment option guidance and decline mitigation

**Confidence:** 0.78

---

### Pain Point 4: First-Time Traveler Trust Deficits — Destination Safety, Cultural Uncertainty, Food/Health Concerns
**Definition:** Indian first-time international travelers experience anxiety about destination safety, cultural protocols, food safety, and health/hygiene — concerns that are often overlooked by generic travel advice designed for Western travelers.

**Evidence & Quotes:**
- "Indian travelers have genuine food concerns that Western travel advice often ignores: vegetarianism, Jain dietary restrictions, spice preferences, hygiene worries"
- First-time travelers need guidance on airport navigation, immigration procedures, local customs, and behavioral rules
- Destination-specific concerns: Bangkok (visa tightening), Dubai (dress code uncertainty), Singapore (strict behavioral rules)

**Implications for booking flow:**
- Travelers need India-specific destination guides integrated into the booking/pre-travel flow
- Trust in the platform increases if it provides guidance tailored to Indian traveler concerns
- Post-booking pre-travel support is critical to reduce anxiety and increase conversion

**Confidence:** 0.76
