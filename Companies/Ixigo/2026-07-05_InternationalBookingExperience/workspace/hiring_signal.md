## Primary Signal
**Can this PM design a genuinely AI-native product experience — not a feature add — that tells a coherent end-to-end story for Indian international travelers, and back it with a credible business case?**

## Evaluation Areas

| Area | Priority | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| AI-native product thinking (not AI features) | PRIMARY | Mentioned 3x in assignment + explicit "not chatbot" constraint — this is the core test |
| End-to-end systems thinking | PRIMARY | "How do all changes come together" — they want a connected narrative, not a feature list |
| Indian customer depth | PRIMARY | "Indian customers" specificity — visa friction, Indian passport, UPI/forex pain, domestic→international behavioral shift |
| Business judgment / prioritization | PRIMARY | "Should ixigo make this a priority? Why/why not?" — they're testing whether you can make a call, not just design |
| Product metrics fluency | PRIMARY | Full section on success measurement + ongoing monitoring — this is a real PM gate |
| Algorithm / personalization awareness | SECONDARY | "Algorithm changes needed" — they want PM who understands ranking, personalization, not just UX |
| Funnel optimization thinking | SECONDARY | Specific ask to revisit flight + hotel booking funnel |
| Rollout strategy | SECONDARY | Asked explicitly — phasing, risk management expected |
| UX design thinking | IMPLICIT | Expected baseline — discovery flow, booking flow — but not the differentiator |

## Seniority Calibration
**Expected level:** PM to Senior PM (no JD, but breadth of scope — discovery + algo + metrics + rollout — is Senior PM territory)

**Implication for output:**
- Depth required: algorithm awareness, not just UX wireframe thinking
- Strategic framing required: "should we do this" judgment call expected
- Metrics: define NPS + conversion + revenue impact, not vanity metrics
- Avoid: junior PM mistake of treating this as a UX exercise only

## Company Type Signals
**Company type:** Consumer OTA, Indian market, public company (listed on NSE/BSE), mid-stage scale

**What they value:**
1. **India-first thinking** — ixigo built its moat on domestic rail/flight for Bharat; they want to know you understand Indian traveler nuances (visa anxiety, forex fear, first-time international traveler)
2. **AI as infrastructure, not decoration** — ixigo has been investing in AI aggressively; they don't want someone who will add "AI suggestions" to existing flows
3. **Business pragmatism** — public company, profitability matters; "should we prioritize?" tests if you understand opportunity cost

## Red Flags to Avoid
1. **Plain AI chatbot solution** — explicitly forbidden; presenting an AI assistant as the solution = automatic reject signal
2. **Generic "improve the UX" answer** — no specifics, no algorithm mention, no India context = shows surface-level thinking
3. **Feature list without prioritization** — 15 features with no stack rank = PM who can't make decisions
4. **Ignoring Indian-specific friction** — not mentioning Indian passport visa complexity, UPI for international payments, rupee → foreign currency anxiety = missed the brief
5. **Weak metrics** — "measure DAU and NPS" without specifics = junior signal

## Success Looks Like
A winning submission presents an AI-native product vision where intelligence is embedded in the journey itself (proactive visa guidance, smart discovery, personalized pricing context), not bolted on as a chat interface. It tells a coherent story from "I want to go somewhere international" to "I'm at the airport" — with ixigo's AI orchestrating each step invisibly. The business case is clear: ixigo's domestic flywheel gives it unique data advantages for international expansion, and the candidate quantifies why this matters for GMV.

## Signal Confidence
**Level:** high-medium
**Reason:** No JD provided, but assignment objectives explicitly state evaluation criteria — rare level of clarity from the hiring team. The "AI-First" emphasis + "not chatbot" constraint makes the primary test unambiguous.
