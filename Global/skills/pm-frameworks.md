# PM Frameworks — Skill Reference

Core product management frameworks for use in research, analysis, case building, and presentation.

---

## 1. Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)

Users hire products to do a job. Focus on the job, not the user persona.

**Format:** "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."

**Use for:** Identifying real user needs beneath stated feature requests; reframing problems.

---

## 2. AARRR — Pirate Metrics (Dave McClure)

| Stage | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Acquisition | How do users find you? (CAC, traffic sources, paid vs organic) |
| Activation | First valuable experience — do they complete the key action? (activation rate) |
| Retention | Do they come back? (D1/D7/D30 retention, churn rate) |
| Revenue | Do they pay? (ARPU, LTV, conversion rate) |
| Referral | Do they bring others? (NPS, K-factor, viral coefficient) |

**Use for:** Diagnosing where a product's growth is leaking; structuring growth strategy assignments.

---

## 3. North Star Metric

One metric that best captures product value delivery. All team work ladders up to it.

**Good NSM properties:** Represents value to user (not vanity), correlates with revenue, measurable in near-real-time, actionable by teams.

**Examples:** Airbnb = Nights booked; Spotify = Time listening; Slack = Messages sent.

**Use for:** Metrics assignments, product strategy, aligning teams.

---

## 4. RICE Prioritization

Score features by: `(Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort`

| Factor | Definition |
|---|---|
| Reach | How many users affected per quarter |
| Impact | Effect per user: 3=massive, 2=high, 1=medium, 0.5=low, 0.25=minimal |
| Confidence | How sure are you: 100%=high, 80%=medium, 50%=low |
| Effort | Person-months of work |

**Use for:** Feature prioritization, roadmap assignments.

---

## 5. MECE — Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive

Structure analysis so that categories don't overlap and together cover everything.

**Test:** Can a finding belong to two categories? (violates ME). Is there a type of finding not captured? (violates CE).

**Use for:** Structuring problem breakdowns, slide organization, framework design.

---

## 6. Porter's Five Forces

Analyze industry competitive dynamics:
1. Threat of new entrants (barriers to entry)
2. Bargaining power of suppliers
3. Bargaining power of buyers
4. Threat of substitutes
5. Competitive rivalry

**Use for:** Market research assignments, competitive strategy, pricing strategy.

---

## 7. The 5 Whys

Ask "Why?" five times to reach root cause rather than symptoms.

**Use for:** Product diagnosis assignments, user problem analysis, root cause of metrics drops.

---

## 8. OKRs — Objectives and Key Results

**Objective:** Qualitative, inspiring direction.  
**Key Results:** 3-5 measurable outcomes that define success. Time-bound.

**Good KR:** Specific, measurable, outcome-focused (not output-focused), ambitious but achievable.

**Use for:** Roadmap assignments, metrics framing, product strategy.

---

## 9. Kano Model

Categorizes features by user satisfaction vs functionality:

| Category | Definition |
|---|---|
| Basic (Must-have) | Expected — absence causes dissatisfaction; presence is neutral |
| Performance | More = more satisfaction (linearly) |
| Excitement (Delighters) | Unexpected — absence is neutral; presence delights |
| Indifferent | Users don't care either way |
| Reverse | Presence causes dissatisfaction for some users |

**Use for:** Feature prioritization, product teardown, user research framing.

---

## 10. Pyramid Principle (Barbara Minto)

Lead with the answer (recommendation), then support with groupings of evidence.

Structure: Answer → Key Arguments (3-ish) → Supporting Evidence per Argument.

**Use for:** Every deck structure, executive memo, recommendation framing.

---

## 11. Hypothesis-Driven Product Thinking

Frame every product decision as a hypothesis:

"We believe [doing X] will cause [users] to [behavior change], resulting in [metric change]. We'll know we're right when [measurable outcome]."

**Use for:** Experiment design assignments, product strategy, validating assumptions.

---

## 12. TAM / SAM / SOM — Market Sizing

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| TAM | Total Addressable Market — entire market if 100% share |
| SAM | Serviceable Addressable Market — portion you can realistically reach |
| SOM | Serviceable Obtainable Market — realistic near-term capture |

**Approaches:** Top-down (industry reports → segment), Bottom-up (unit × price × frequency).

**Use for:** Market research, growth strategy, pricing assignments.

---

## 13. Outcome vs Output Distinction

**Output:** What you shipped (feature, page, API).  
**Outcome:** The behavior change or metric movement it caused.

Always frame recommendations around outcomes ("increase D7 retention by 15%"), not outputs ("build a notification system").

---

## 14. The 2x2 Prioritization Matrix

Plot initiatives on two axes (typically impact vs effort, or urgency vs importance).

Quadrants: Quick wins (high impact, low effort) → do first. Big bets (high impact, high effort) → plan carefully. Fill-ins (low impact, low effort) → do if bandwidth. Avoid (low impact, high effort).

---

## 15. Experiment Design (A/B Test Framework)

**Components:** Hypothesis, metric (primary + guardrail), treatment vs control, sample size, duration, success threshold, rollout plan.

**Guardrails:** Metrics that must not degrade during the experiment (e.g., revenue per user for a retention experiment).

**Use for:** Metrics/analytics assignments, growth strategy, feature validation.
