# Hiring Signal Patterns — Skill Reference

What companies are actually evaluating in interview assignments, beyond the stated question.

---

## How to Read a Hiring Signal

The assignment text reveals the surface task. The hiring signal is what the evaluator will score you on. They are often different.

**Process:**
1. Read the JD — what does this role actually require day-to-day?
2. Read the assignment — what type of thinking does it require?
3. Read the interviewer profile — what do they value?
4. Identify the gap between surface task and underlying evaluation criteria

## Common Evaluation Areas by Assignment Type

### Product Strategy / Growth Strategy
- **Signals:** Structured thinking, market understanding, prioritization rigor, business sense, clarity on tradeoffs
- **What they want to see:** A clear "so what," not just a list of ideas. Show you know what NOT to do.
- **Failure mode:** Listing 15 ideas with no prioritization. Being generic.

### Product Teardown / Critique
- **Signals:** User empathy, analytical depth, business model understanding, opinionated POV
- **What they want to see:** A specific, defensible recommendation, not "it depends." Show you have taste.
- **Failure mode:** Describing features instead of evaluating them. Praising everything.

### PRD / Product Spec
- **Signals:** Requirements thinking, edge case awareness, cross-functional consideration, definition of done
- **What they want to see:** Clear success metrics, rollout plan, engineering tradeoffs acknowledged
- **Failure mode:** Writing a wishlist. Missing metrics, risks, or rollout considerations.

### Metrics / Diagnostics
- **Signals:** Hypothesis-driven thinking, data literacy, structured diagnosis, understanding of instrumentation
- **What they want to see:** A tree of possible causes, explicit hypotheses, what data you'd look at and why
- **Failure mode:** Listing metrics without framing hypotheses. Not knowing what to instrument.

### Market Research / Competitive Analysis
- **Signals:** Research depth, synthesis quality, strategic implication of findings
- **What they want to see:** Insights (patterns + implications), not just facts. "So what?"
- **Failure mode:** Listing company features. Not connecting research to a recommendation.

### Experiment Design / A/B Testing
- **Signals:** Statistical understanding, hypothesis precision, guardrail awareness, rollout discipline
- **What they want to see:** Correct sample size logic, clear guardrail metrics, staged rollout plan
- **Failure mode:** Missing guardrail metrics. Not specifying success threshold.

### Executive Memo / Business Case
- **Signals:** Executive communication, decisiveness, business sense, conciseness
- **What they want to see:** Clear recommendation up front. No burying the lede.
- **Failure mode:** Too long. Too many caveats. No clear ask.

## Seniority Signal Calibration

### APM / Junior PM
- Expected: Clear thinking, structured communication, user empathy, learning orientation
- Evaluated on: Logic and structure, not necessarily depth of business experience

### PM (3-5 years)
- Expected: Prioritization rigor, metrics fluency, cross-functional awareness, independent recommendations
- Evaluated on: Do they have a point of view? Can they handle ambiguity?

### Senior PM (5-8 years)
- Expected: Strategic thinking, business impact framing, stakeholder management, tradeoff navigation
- Evaluated on: Business sense, not just product instincts. Can they connect product to revenue?

### Principal / Group PM / Director
- Expected: Portfolio thinking, org-level influence, company-level impact, executive communication
- Evaluated on: Are they thinking about the right problem? Can they say no?

## Company Type Signals

### Consumer / B2C
- Care about: User psychology, growth levers, retention mechanics, emotional resonance
- Style: Data-informed, user-centric, move fast

### Fintech / Payments
- Care about: Trust, compliance awareness, activation friction, business model clarity
- Style: Rigorous, risk-aware, metrics-heavy

### B2B SaaS
- Care about: Customer success lens, enterprise buying process, NRR thinking, integrations
- Style: Structured, business-case-driven, implementation-aware

### Marketplace / Platform
- Care about: Supply/demand balance, network effects, liquidity thinking, multi-sided dynamics
- Style: Systems thinking, unit economics, growth mechanics

### E-commerce / Travel / OTA
- Care about: Conversion, booking funnel, loyalty, seasonal dynamics, competitive pricing
- Style: A/B test culture, funnel metrics, commercial acumen

### Startup (<100 people)
- Care about: Resourcefulness, founder-mode thinking, doing more with less, speed
- Style: Opinionated, practical, no fluff

## What NOT to Optimize For

- **Making the output look pretty** — content > design at this stage
- **Covering everything** — depth on the right things > breadth on all things
- **Sounding impressive** — clarity beats sophistication
- **Agreeing with the premise** — challenge it if the premise is wrong (shows confidence)
- **Generic frameworks** — a generic Porter's Five Forces is less impressive than a specific, evidence-backed analysis

---

## Red Flags (what gets a "No" from interviewers)

- No clear recommendation (excessive hedging)
- No metrics / numbers cited (even estimates)
- Assumptions not stated
- Ignoring engineering / feasibility tradeoffs
- No prioritization (just a list)
- No "so what" — data without implication
- Polished formatting with shallow thinking underneath
