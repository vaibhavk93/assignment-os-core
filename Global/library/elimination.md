# Elimination

Loaded on a trigger match from `INDEX.md`.

> **The rule this whole file exists for: ranking is not elimination.**
> RICE, ICE, weighted scoring and impact/effort 2×2s **order** an option set; they never remove anything. Worse, they are *compensatory* — a high score on one attribute offsets a fatal failure on another, which is how an option that violates a hard constraint survives with a respectable number next to it. Eliminate first, on non-compensatory grounds. Rank only the survivors.

---

## Conjunctive screening — *the one to reach for first*
**Trigger:** a constraint is real and **cannot be traded off** — regulatory, legal, latency, budget, platform.
**Source:** non-compensatory choice rules, decision science (conjunctive / lexicographic). `peer-reviewed`

Set a hard threshold per constraint. **One failure eliminates the option outright**, no matter how strong it is elsewhere. No score, no weighting, no discussion.

Write the surviving set *and* the eliminated set with the constraint each one died on. The eliminated list is what proves the decision was made rather than assumed — an interviewer asking "did you consider X?" gets the constraint that killed X, not a shrug.

**Worked example:** a research platform that is not a registered investment adviser cannot ship buy recommendations, ranked "top picks", or price targets. That is a gate, not a tradeoff — every option containing one is out before feasibility or effort is even discussed.

## Elimination by Aspects
**Trigger:** more than ~7 options, weakly differentiated, and compensatory scoring is just laundering a preference you already hold.
**Source:** Tversky 1972, *Psychological Review*. `peer-reviewed`

Pick the single most important attribute → cut everything below threshold → pick the next attribute → repeat until few enough to reason about properly. Order of attributes determines the outcome, so state the order and why.

## APEASE
**Trigger:** you have generated intervention options (usually via COM-B) and must cut them.
**Source:** Michie et al., Behaviour Change Wheel. `peer-reviewed` — the most rigorous eliminator in the behavioural literature.

Score each option against six criteria; any that fails outright is cut:
**A**ffordability · **P**racticability · **E**ffectiveness / cost-effectiveness · **A**cceptability (to users *and* stakeholders) · **S**ide-effects and safety · **E**quity.

Side-effects and equity are the two nobody applies unprompted, and the two that most often catch real harm.

## Kano
**Trigger:** cutting features that add cost without adding satisfaction.
**Source:** Kano et al. `peer-reviewed`
Must-be · one-dimensional · attractive · **indifferent** · **reverse**.
The elimination value is entirely in the last two: *indifferent* features cost and return nothing, *reverse* features make things actively worse for some segment. Both should be cut, and are usually the ones a team is most attached to.

## MoSCoW — the *Won't* half
**Trigger:** scope must shrink and nobody will say aloud what's being dropped.
**Source:** DSDM. `practitioner`
Must · Should · Could · **Won't-this-time**. The first three are a ranking; only "Won't" eliminates, and it is the one teams skip. An unfilled Won't column means nothing was actually cut.

## Pre-mortem
**Trigger:** a plan everyone already agrees with.
**Source:** Klein, HBR 2007. `peer-reviewed`
Assume it is twelve months later and the thing failed. Write the obituary. Working backwards from assumed failure surfaces objections that consensus suppresses — agreement is the signal to run it, not a reason to skip it.

## Assumption mapping / Riskiest Assumption Test
**Trigger:** eliminating options whose riskiest assumption cannot be tested inside your timeline.
**Source:** Bland & Osterwalder, *Testing Business Ideas*. `practitioner`
Plot assumptions on importance × evidence. The high-importance / low-evidence quadrant is what to test first — and an option whose riskiest assumption is *untestable* within the horizon should be cut rather than carried.

## Cost of Delay ÷ Duration (CD3)
**Trigger:** sequencing under a fixed team where delay carries quantifiable economic cost.
**Source:** Reinertsen, *Principles of Product Development Flow*. `practitioner`
Sequences rather than eliminates — included here because it is the correct tool when the real question is *order*, and people reach for RICE instead.
