# Archetypes

Loaded on a trigger match from `INDEX.md`. Problem *types* are finite; companies, markets and constraints are infinite. Classify on the finite axis, then treat the company as input.

> **Classify on both axes. Do not collapse them.**
> The consulting canon indexes on the **business decision**; the PM canon indexes on the **competency being scored**. They are orthogonal, and a single brief sits on both — "why did this metric drop" is a *profitability* problem and an *execution* competency simultaneously. Collapsing them loses whichever axis you dropped, and it's usually the competency one — which is the axis the interviewer is actually grading.

---

## Axis 1 — Business decision
**Trigger:** the brief names a business decision — *should we enter / launch / price / cut / respond*.
**Source:** Cosentino, *Case in Point* (Ivy Case System) — 12 types. `practitioner`

New market entry · industry analysis · M&A · new product development · pricing · growth strategy · starting a new business · competitive response · increasing sales · reducing costs · increasing profits · turnaround.

**Cheng's compression** (*LOMS*, `practitioner`): four recombine to cover most of the above — profitability · business situation (new product / market / competitor) · M&A · special — plus two building blocks used inside them: market sizing and breakeven. Useful when the brief spans several of the twelve.

---

## Axis 2 — PM competency
**Trigger:** you need to know which skill the interviewer is scoring.
**Source:** Lin, *Decode and Conquer*; Exponent's taxonomy. `practitioner`

Product design · technical · estimation / market sizing · pricing · metrics & execution · tradeoff · A/B testing · market entry · strategy · product vision · behavioural · take-home.

This axis determines what "good" looks like more than the business axis does. A product-design brief and a strategy brief about the same feature are graded on completely different things — the first on user reasoning and progressive disclosure, the second on market logic and defensibility.

Named sub-frameworks from this canon (`practitioner`, all): **CIRCLES** for design questions, **AARM** for metrics, **DIGS** for behavioural. Use them to think; never name them — see `pm-frameworks`.

## Taxonomies are contested — treat as overlapping, not clean
No canonical, non-overlapping list of case types exists. The counts differ by source (12 / 4 / ~10 / 8) because each vendor cuts the space differently, and all the vendor taxonomies are `practitioner`-tier at best. Use these as a **checklist for what you might be missing**, never as evidence, and never claim "there are N types of case" in a deliverable.

---

## How to use this in practice
1. Classify on both axes and write it down in one line.
2. Business axis → what the *answer* must contain (market logic, unit economics, competitive response).
3. Competency axis → what the *deliverable* is graded on (see `assignment-type-templates` for emphasis and `checker-rubrics` for the per-type gates).
4. If the two axes disagree about what matters, the competency axis wins — it's the one being scored.
